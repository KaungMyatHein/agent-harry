# Product Positioner

`product-positioner` · **Phase:** Define · **Model:** sonnet · **Decision authority:** propose

### Hooks
- **Hook A —** Five stakeholders, five different answers to "what is this product?" That's not alignment — that's a launch about to wobble.
- **Hook B —** Walk away with one sharp sentence that says exactly what you are, who it's for, and why it's different.
- **Hook C —** The fastest way to make a product forgettable is to make it for everyone. So — who isn't it for?

## TL;DR
This is the agent that sharpens what a product, feature, or release IS and ISN'T. It picks a side: clear positioning statements, value props, a differentiation narrative, and naming — instead of mushy "all things to all people" messaging.

## What it does
It writes positioning statements: the crisp definition of who the product is for, what it does, and why it beats the alternative.

It crafts value propositions — turning a list of capabilities into the reason a specific person should care.

It builds the differentiation narrative: the story of how you're genuinely not-the-same, so the market remembers you for something specific instead of nothing in particular.

And it helps with naming, so what you call a product or feature actually carries its meaning. As a "propose" agent, it brings a sharp, opinionated draft and a recommendation — then leaves the final call with you.

## When to reach for it
- Stakeholders are pulled in different directions about scope or message.
- You need to define not just what a release IS, but what it deliberately ISN'T.
- You're naming a product or feature and want the name to do real work.

## Inputs → Outputs
| It needs | It produces |
|---|---|
| The product/feature/release, its audience, and the competitive context | Positioning statements, value props, a differentiation narrative, and naming options |

## Where it sits in the pipeline
It works in the Define phase, downstream of the pm-strategist's strategic frame and informed by the competitive-analyst's category read. Its sharpened positioning gives the feature-prioritizer and the delivery agents a clear "what this is" to build against. Being a "propose" agent, its recommendations route through you before they ship.
