# Discovery Researcher

`discovery-researcher` · **Phase:** Discovery · **Model:** sonnet · **Decision authority:** autonomous

### Hooks
- **Hook A —** You have a Notion full of interviews and a survey nobody read. None of it has become an insight yet.
- **Hook B —** Walk into the room knowing the real problem — backed by evidence, not the loudest opinion.
- **Hook C —** What if the feature everyone's fighting over is solving a problem your users never had?

## TL;DR
This is the agent that refuses to skip ahead to solutions. It turns raw research — interviews, surveys, analytics, secondary sources, or piles of old artifacts — into a clearly framed problem you can actually build against.

## What it does
It synthesizes user interviews into themes and patterns, so a dozen conversations become a handful of clear findings instead of a wall of quotes.

It analyzes the data you already have — survey results, product analytics, support tickets — and pulls signal out of the noise. It also does secondary research when you need to understand a space you're new to.

It frames problems properly: turning a vague "users are unhappy" into a sharp, testable statement of who hurts, when, and why. It runs JTBD discovery to surface the job people are really hiring your product to do.

And when you already have research sitting in files or Notion, it does meta-analysis — reading across prior artifacts to find what they collectively say (and where they disagree).

## When to reach for it
- You're starting a new feature and want to understand the problem before anyone sketches a solution.
- Stakeholders disagree about what the problem even is.
- You have data — interviews, surveys, analytics — that has never been turned into insight.

## Inputs → Outputs
| It needs | It produces |
|---|---|
| Raw research (interviews, surveys, analytics) or a problem space to investigate | Synthesized themes, a framed problem statement, JTBD insights, and an evidence trail |

## Where it sits in the pipeline
It runs at the very front of the Discovery phase, often before anything else. Agent Harry's Research-First Gate is built around exactly this: no solutioning until the problem is grounded in evidence. Its framed problem feeds the rest of Discovery and Define — including the competitive-analyst and the pm-strategist.
